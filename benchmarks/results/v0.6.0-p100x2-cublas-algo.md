# v0.6.0 on 2x P100 — cuBLAS gemm algorithm on GP100 (patch 51)

Ported from [Kmic-68/llama.cpp](https://github.com/Kmic-68/llama.cpp), commit `fccdafca1`
(`p100-optimizations`, OPTLOG attempt 153). Measured here on 2026-10-13 before shipping it, and it is
**on by default**: it is both faster at the shapes where the default picks the wrong kernel and an
order of magnitude closer to fp32.

Machine `x99dual`: 2x Tesla P100-PCIE-16GB, build line `d81235049 (11429)` = llama.cpp `v0.6.0`,
46-patch tree. Stock arm `~/llama.cpp-v0.6.0`, patched arm `~/llama.cpp-v0.6.0-p100`.

## Method

One build, two arms. `algo6` is the patched binary with its default (ALGO6); `tensor_op` is the same
binary through a wrapper that exports `GGML_CUDA_CUBLAS_ALGO_PASCAL=-1`, which is upstream's
`CUBLAS_GEMM_DEFAULT_TENSOR_OP`. Five rounds, arms interleaved with the order reversed on even rounds,
cards cooled to 45 C between configs (`-t 28 -ub 2048 -fa on -n 128`). As usual the csv `env` column
reads `none` because the switch lives in the wrapper, not the runner.

The `tensor_op` arm reproduces the previous session's shipped numbers to two decimals — pp512 242.92
against 242.88, tg128 33.63 against 33.63 — so this is the same arm measured twice, not two builds
that happen to agree.

## Speed

Qwen3.8-27B Q4_0, `-sm tensor`, 38-60 C:

| test | upstream algo | ALGO6 | |
|---|---:|---:|---:|
| pp512 | 242.92 ± 0.53 | **369.04 ± 4.19** | **+51.9%** |
| pp1024 | 306.73 ± 1.86 | **396.02 ± 3.20** | **+29.1%** |
| pp2048 | 402.66 ± 1.19 | 399.36 ± 2.33 | −0.8% |
| tg128 | 33.63 ± 0.05 | 33.60 ± 0.04 | −0.1% |

Ornith-1.0-35B-A3B UD-Q4_K_M, `-sm tensor`, `-p 4096`, 44-54 C: pp4096 +0.61%, tg128 +0.51% — level,
in both cases inside the round-to-round spread.

Decode does not move, which is the expected shape: a decode step is a one-row matvec that never
reaches this call. The gain is confined to prefill matmuls of 512-1024 rows, and it stops at 2048.

## Accuracy

Same model, `llama-perplexity`, wikitext-2 `wiki.test.raw`, 20 chunks of `-c 4096` = 81,920 tokens,
`-ub 2048`, three arms over identical tokens (the `±` is the tool's 95% CI over the corpus, so it is
far larger than the gaps below — the comparison is paired, not independent):

| arm | matmul compute | PPL | distance from the fp32 reference |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| `tensor_op` | fp16, long-chain accumulation | 6.2785 ± 0.07889 | +0.0140 |
| `algo6` | fp16, blocked accumulation | 6.2658 ± 0.07887 | **+0.0013** |
| `fp32ref` | `GGML_CUDA_CUBLAS_COMPUTE_TYPE=f32` | 6.2645 ± 0.07885 | — |

ALGO6 sits **10.8× closer to the all-fp32 matmul reference** than the algorithm it replaces, and in
the same direction — it moves toward fp32 rather than wandering. Per-chunk values agree: the first
chunk reads 7.1659 / 7.1175 / 7.1159 across the three arms, in that order.

This matches the source fork's claim on a different model and quantization (they measured Q6_K, and
reported NMSE against an fp64 product of the same fp16 inputs dropping from ~7e-5 to 1.3e-5). Our
metric is not theirs: we compared against an fp32-compute run of the same model, not against fp64, so
"10x" is the same magnitude arrived at differently.

## What was taken from the fork and what was not

- Taken: this one call site. The fork also has `a7cdad458`, which requests **ALGO4 for the attention
  PV product** (3.4× lower error); that lives in their own fold-attention kernels, which we do not
  have, so there is nothing here to apply it to.
- Not taken: their `55e496262` reverted an **ALGO3 for wide GEMMs**, "reassociated with no precision
  argument behind it" — worth keeping as policy: an algorithm change needs an accuracy claim, not just
  a timing win. Their own measurement of ALGO5: same accuracy as ALGO6, 2.5-3.5% slower.
- Range: ALGO7-23 are unsupported for `COMPUTE_16F`, so the env parser caps at 6 and the call retries
  with upstream's default on any failure.

## Limits

Two models, one quantization each, and both Q4_K_M/Q4_0 rather than the fork's Q6_K. The speed win is
at 512 and 1024 prompt tokens; `-ub 2048` prefill is level, so a workload that only ever runs large
prefill batches sees the accuracy change and not the time. No KLD or logit-level number here —
`ppl.sh`-style pairing gives one scalar per arm, which is enough to show the direction and rule out a
catastrophe, not enough to characterise the difference.
